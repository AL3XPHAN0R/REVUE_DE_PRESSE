# Persona & Target Audience (Layer 3 — Reference)

## Author
Alexandre Phanor — future licensed personal insurance broker (life, disability, critical illness — LLQP/PQAP) in Quebec, Canada. Bilingual, French-first. Creating Instagram and podcast content that demystifies income protection and mortgage protection for French-Canadian healthcare professionals, self-employed workers and young homeowning households.

**Content mission:** own the *problems* of income protection and mortgage protection before the solution is needed — not product-selling content, but problem-owning content. The audience should think of Alexandre when the problem surfaces, not when they shop for insurance.

## The niches — a mix of four target groups (revised 2026-11-02)

Source: the "Niches de protection" and "Niches entrepreneurs" analysis sheets — the four
porte A niches **Invalidité en pratique privée**, **Invalidité du propriétaire**,
**Assurance prêt** and **Assurance prêt des autonomes**. Each niche is *a product + a
precise group + a trigger event*.

**Relevance comes first.** The digest serves a mix of the four groups below, but no group
has a quota: pick the most relevant articles, then use group variety only as a tie-breaker.

1. **Healthcare professionals in private practice** (main disability target) — psychologists, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech therapists, kinesiologists, massage therapists, foot-care and aesthetic nurses, nurse practitioners in private clinics. Includes those leaving the public network for private practice. Often covered only by their professional order's or association's plan.
2. **Self-employed workers and small-business owners** (1 to 10 employees, incorporated or not) — consultants, tradespeople, shopkeepers, professionals, including healthcare professionals who incorporate or open a clinic. Typically **no group coverage at all**: if their income stops, the whole household is hit.
3. **Young homeowning households** — couples aged 25 to 45 with a mortgage, especially $500,000 and up, buying or renewing; including self-employed buyers.
4. **Public health-network employees with coverage gaps** (secondary) — CISSS/CIUSSS employees whose group LTD leaves a gap a complement can fill. The tone rule below applies to this group above all.

Out of scope for now: seniors and pre-retirees (porte B), established SMEs (porte B).

## Trigger events — what makes an article timely for these groups
- Leaving a salaried job / the public network (end of group coverage), opening a private practice or clinic, incorporating, hiring a first employee
- Changes to a professional order's or association's insurance plan
- Buying a home, mortgage renewal (every ~5 years), refinancing (including to launch or grow a business)
- Birth of a child

Articles that document these moments (statistics, trends, policy changes, real cases) are high-value even without an insurance angle.

## Tone rule — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Position everything as **complementing what people already have**, never as exposing a weakness in it.

- **Public-network employees (unionized):** they are proud of the coverage they bargained for. Never frame the group plan as inadequate and never criticize unions or negotiated coverage. Getting this wrong closes the entire market.
- **Order / association plans:** state their characteristics factually (premiums that rise with age, conditions the insurer can change, coverage lost on leaving the order) as things an individual policy addresses — never as a scandal.
- **Bank / lender creditor insurance:** compare factually (who owns the policy, who the beneficiary is, level vs declining benefit, portability, underwriting at issue vs at claim). No fear-mongering about lenders.

Reject articles framed as "your group plan is failing you", "group benefits are inadequate", or that attack unions or negotiated coverage.

## Product scope (the problems the content owns)

1. **Individual disability insurance**
   - *Private practice:* replacing self-employment income; limits of order/association plans; proving income (notices of assessment, financial statements); waiting periods; own-occupation definitions.
   - *Business owners:* replacing the owner's income whether paid as salary or dividends; who pays the premium (the person or the corporation) changes the tax on the benefit; low benefit caps in small group plans leave well-paid owners under-insured; start-ups with no income history.
   - *Public employees:* DI top-up above group LTD — benefit caps, offsets against RRQ/CNESST/SAAQ, taxable vs non-taxable benefit, the "own occupation" → "any occupation" switch at 24 months.
2. **Mortgage protection outside the bank (assurance prêt)** — individually owned T20/T30 term life plus disability and critical illness on the mortgage, instead of the lender's creditor insurance: level vs declining benefit, family as beneficiary rather than the lender, portability when switching lenders, underwriting at issue rather than at claim. Stronger need for self-employed buyers with no group coverage behind them.
3. **Critical illness** — sold with the mortgage and disability package; the cost gap a diagnosis opens for a household or a solo business.

## Target audience — themes

### Direct themes (the obvious problem each product solves)
- Disability: incidence, leading causes (mental health, burnout, musculoskeletal), claims experience, length of absences, insurer claim practices
- Self-employed and owners' income protection: no group coverage, proving income, salary vs dividends, incorporation
- Private practice in healthcare: growth of private clinics, professionals moving from public to private, order/association insurance plans
- Mortgage life and disability insurance: bank creditor insurance vs individually owned term; mortgage renewals, first-time buyers, refinancing
- Critical illness: diagnosis rates, the financial gap it opens, survey data on coverage
- Public-employee group LTD and how individual coverage complements it; RRQ, CNESST, SAAQ and RQAP interaction with disability and leave

### Indirect themes (adjacent angles — same problem, no product mention)
- **Healthcare workforce:** staffing shortage, TSO, absenteeism and disability rates, the shift toward private practice and private clinics
- **Self-employment economy:** growth of self-employment and solo incorporations, gig/contract work, financial fragility of solo businesses
- **Household finance:** mortgage debt and renewal shock, affordability, first-time buyers, emergency savings, household financial fragility
- **Women's financial reality:** career interruptions, maternity and RQAP (including for the self-employed), income gaps
- **Tax and savings for these groups:** REER/CELI/CELIAPP, taxes of the self-employed, retirement saving without a workplace pension (as what a protection client opens up next)
- **Psychology:** optimism bias about disability, "it won't happen to me", the "I'm already covered" assumption, low-probability/high-impact risk

### Regulatory & industry
- AMF / CSF / CIRO regulation affecting personal insurance distribution (Quebec and Canada)
- Creditor insurance regulation and enforcement — bank mortgage insurance sales practices
- Individual disability, critical illness and term life market: product changes, pricing, underwriting trends, insurer results that touch these products
- Insurtech/fintech trends affecting individual coverage and advice

## Selection priority
- **Relevance first.** Direct themes and regulatory/industry items are the daily backbone of the digest.
- Indirect themes add variety and podcast-worthy angles but should stay a minority of the 5–8 articles (roughly no more than half).
- First tie-breaker: **variety across the four target groups** — when relevance is comparable, avoid a digest that speaks to only one group.
- Second tie-breaker: prefer evergreen, podcast-worthy angles — concrete stories, case studies, surprising statistics — over generic or purely descriptive coverage. Ask whether the article still supports a video six months from now.
- Third tie-breaker: prefer Quebec and French-Canadian material over generic Canadian or US coverage.

## Exclusion criteria — EXCLUDE articles about
- General politics with no insurance, income-protection, mortgage or healthcare-workforce angle
- B2C mass-market P&C insurance (auto, home, travel)
- **Overhead expense insurance (assurance frais généraux)** — out of scope for now (decided 2026-11-02)
- Porte B business insurance: key-person, buy-sell / shareholder agreements, corporate-owned permanent life, business succession and estate freezes
- Business news that does not touch the owner's personal income, protection or mortgage: strategy, funding rounds, M&A, business valuation, company earnings
- Group benefits written from the employer's or HR buyer's perspective — plan design as a business decision rather than as coverage the member relies on (group insurance also requires a different AMF certificate)
- Corporate/enterprise risk and commercial P&C (property, liability, cyber)
- Generic macroeconomic news (inflation, interest rates) unless tied to household finances, mortgages or the self-employed
- Pure investment/portfolio/markets content with no protection angle; DB pension-fund investment news
- Product marketing or carrier press releases with no editorial substance
- Advisor-industry people moves, awards and events
- Clinical or medical-practice news (treatments, research findings) with no financial, coverage or workforce angle
- Anything whose framing attacks unions or negotiated group coverage (see tone rule)
- Seniors' products (funeral, snowbird travel, estate tax, long-term care) — porte B, out of scope for now
