# Persona & Target Audience (Layer 3 — Reference)

## Author
Alexandre Phanor — future licensed personal insurance broker (life, disability, critical illness — LLQP/PQAP) in Quebec, Canada. Bilingual, French-first. Creating Instagram and podcast content that demystifies personal insurance for French-Canadian healthcare workers.

**Content mission:** own the *problems* of income protection, mortgage protection and retirement literacy before the solution is needed — not product-selling content, but problem-owning content. The audience should think of Alexandre when the problem surfaces, not when they shop for insurance.

## The niche — French-Canadian healthcare workers

Chosen because the segment is large, resistant to technological displacement, takes in a steady stream of new entrants every year, pays well, and is women-dominated.

**Scope:** healthcare workers across French-speaking Canada. The Quebec public network (CISSS/CIUSSS — nurses, préposés aux bénéficiaires, technologists, professionals) is the dense core, plus private clinics, pharmacies, CHSLD privés, and Franco-Ontarian and Acadian healthcare workers.

Four structural features shape what is worth covering:

1. **Group coverage is collectively negotiated.** A small number of standardized plan documents rather than thousands of unique ones — one deep analysis serves an entire union. Articles about a specific negotiated plan or a specific union's coverage are therefore high-value, not narrow.
2. **The pension is a defined benefit plan.** RREGOP in the Quebec public network, HOOPP for Franco-Ontarians, comparable public plans elsewhere. This substantially changes what these people actually need, and most brokers don't understand it — so DB pension mechanics deserve space even when the article carries no insurance angle.
3. **Shift work.** Content has to be useful at 3 a.m., which favours evergreen material over time-sensitive events. Prefer articles that can anchor an evergreen video months from now over articles that expire in a week.
4. **Dense internal referral networks.** Units talk and one good experience travels, so credibility and accuracy matter more than volume.

## Tone rule — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Position everything as **complementing what they have bargained for**, never as exposing a weakness in it. These are unionized workers who are proud of their plan. Getting this wrong closes the entire market.

Applied to selection: prefer articles that let Alexandre build on top of the group plan or explain how it works. **Reject** articles framed as "your group plan is failing you", "group benefits are inadequate", or that criticize unions or negotiated coverage. Where an article documents a genuine limit of a group plan, it is usable only if that limit can be stated factually — as something a complement addresses, never as a flaw in what the union won.

## Product scope (the 5 problems the content owns)

1. **Individual critical illness** — group plans rarely include meaningful CI; a diagnosis brings costs the plan does not reimburse
2. **Life beyond the group multiple** — group life is often ~1x salary and ends at retirement; conversion privileges, portability, coverage that outlives the job
3. **DI top-up above LTD** — benefit caps, offsets against RRQ/CNESST/SAAQ, taxable vs non-taxable benefit, and the "own occupation" → "any occupation" switch at 24 months
4. **Mortgage protection outside the bank** — replacing bank creditor insurance (assurance prêt hypothécaire) with individually owned T20/T30 term life, and term disability paired with the mortgage: level vs declining benefit, family as beneficiary rather than the lender, portability when switching lenders, underwriting at issue rather than at claim
5. **DB pension literacy** — RREGOP and equivalents: RRQ coordination at 65, bridge benefit, actuarial reduction on early retirement, service buy-backs, survivor options

## Target audience

**Primary:**
- Nurses, infirmières auxiliaires, préposés aux bénéficiaires (PAB)
- Medical technologists, radiology and lab technicians, respiratory therapists
- Professionals: physiotherapists, occupational therapists, social workers, pharmacists and pharmacy technicians
- Administrative and support staff in the health network
- Early-career entrants (new grads, first mortgage, first permanent position) and mid-career workers 10–20 years from retirement
- Predominantly women, often on shift schedules and carrying household financial responsibility

**Secondary (referral / trust network — content to share WITH them):**
- Union locals and their benefits committees
- Mortgage brokers, notaries, and financial planners serving the same clientele

### Direct themes (the obvious problem each product solves)
- Group insurance in the health network: invalidité/LTD, coverage limits, claims experience, plan changes
- Critical illness and the cost gap it opens for a salaried household
- Mortgage life and disability insurance: bank creditor insurance vs individually owned term (T20/T30)
- RREGOP and other DB pension mechanics: coordination, bridge, buy-backs, early retirement, survivor benefits
- RRQ, CNESST and RQAP interaction with disability, injury and leave
- Disability incidence among healthcare workers — mental health, burnout and musculoskeletal claims as leading causes
- Life and disability coverage gaps among Canadian employees; what group coverage does and doesn't reach

### Indirect themes (adjacent angles — same problem, no product mention)
- **Healthcare workforce:** staffing shortage, TSO / mandatory overtime, retention, absenteeism and disability rates in the network
- **Shift work and health:** night-shift health effects, sleep, chronic disease and cancer risk
- **Women's financial reality:** career interruptions, maternity and RQAP, longevity, retirement income gaps
- **Household finance:** mortgage debt and renewal shock, first-time buyers, household financial fragility, emergency savings
- **Retirement:** decumulation, deferring RRQ to 70, REER/CELI/CELIAPP, phased retirement, retiring from a DB plan
- **Bargaining context:** collective agreement negotiations affecting benefits or pension — report factually, see the tone rule
- **Psychology:** optimism bias about disability, the "I'm already covered at work" assumption, low-probability/high-impact risk

### Regulatory & industry
- AMF / CSF / ChAD / CIRO regulation affecting personal insurance distribution (Quebec and Canada)
- Creditor insurance regulation and enforcement — bank mortgage insurance sales practices
- Retraite Québec and public pension policy
- Insurtech/fintech trends affecting individual coverage and advice

## Selection priority
- Direct themes and regulatory/industry items come first — they are the daily backbone of the digest.
- Indirect themes add variety and podcast-worthy story angles but should stay a minority of the 5–8 articles selected (roughly no more than half), not overtake the direct/product-adjacent content.
- Tie-breaker when relevance is otherwise similar: prefer evergreen, podcast-worthy angles — concrete stories, case studies, or surprising statistics — over generic or purely descriptive coverage. Ask whether the article still supports a video six months from now.
- Second tie-breaker: prefer Quebec and French-Canadian material over generic Canadian or US coverage.

## Exclusion criteria — EXCLUDE articles about
- General politics with no insurance, pension, or healthcare-workforce angle
- B2C mass-market P&C insurance (auto, home, travel)
- Business-owner insurance: overhead expense, key-person, buy-sell, shareholder agreements
- SME ownership, entrepreneurship, business succession, business valuation (retired 2026-09-03 — market pivot from SME owners to employed healthcare workers)
- Group benefits written from the employer's or HR buyer's perspective — plan design as a business decision rather than as coverage the member relies on
- Corporate/enterprise risk and commercial P&C (property, liability, cyber)
- Generic macroeconomic news (inflation, interest rates) unless tied to household finances, mortgages, or pensions
- Pure investment/portfolio/markets content with no protection, pension, or retirement-income angle
- Product marketing or carrier press releases with no editorial substance
- Clinical or medical-practice news (treatments, research findings, health system reform) with no financial, coverage, or workforce angle
- Anything whose framing attacks unions or negotiated group coverage (see tone rule)
- Topics with no clear relevance to French-Canadian healthcare workers' income protection, mortgage protection, or retirement
